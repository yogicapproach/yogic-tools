Source: the Practice Guide, printed pp. 25–261. The corpus states the same split for PM 3 in Understanding Yoga Vidya7.

  • Mantra. The corpus does not name a mantra for PM 1, 2 or 3. It describes a step called mantra awareness: in the last five of the 25 movements you repeat “the mantra of the chakra” silently89. The 2015 and Series 4 books state that the specific chakras and mantras are left out on purpose, to be learnt in the presence of a teacher10.
  • How to repeat it. Repeat the mantra while exhaling; while inhaling, breathe in its vibrations11.
  • Seed mantras. The corpus states the Bihar School seed mantras: Lam for mooladhara and Ram for manipura12. These appear in other books, not for PM, so they are background.
  • Each practice has five steps, five movements each: muscle movement, breath, visualisation, chakra, mantra13.
  • A caution. Chakra and mantra awareness is for personal practice, not for general teaching14.
In short. The corpus states that PM 1 uses mooladhara, PM 2 uses manipura, and PM 3 uses manipura for the four straight-leg asanas and mooladhara for the four squatting ones (Practice Guide, printed pp. 25–26). It describes the chakra as a swirl of energy, not a flat picture; for manipura, as churning like nauli. It does not name a mantra: the practice calls for “the mantra of the chakra” in the last five movements, and the specific mantras are left to be learnt from a teacher.

How to visualise the chakra

Not a two-dimensional picture of petals, letters and Ganesha, but a swirl of energy. For manipura, a churning. In the wider teaching of the same books: a point of light or a lotus, seen in chidakasha, the inner space.

2

“When it comes to chakra, how do you visualize a chakra? As a two-dimensional photograph or a swirl of energy? It has to be seen as a swirl of energy, not as a two-dimensional image of the four-petalled mooladhara with letters and the image of Ganesha. It is not that visualization that you do here, but you see the swirl of energy in mooladhara. You see the vortex of prana shakti in the form of a swirl going round and round.”

A Practice Guide, p. 14 · PDF p. 28 · ✓ checked against the page
5

“In this practice, the chakra to be focused on is manipura. However, it is not a static image of manipura, you have to see manipura churning. It is like nauli happening in the region of manipura. That is the visualization. As you visualize manipura churn, it creates a sound or a hum due to the movement.”

A Practice Guide, p. 27 · PDF p. 41 · ✓ checked against the page

What APMB itself says about the Pawanmuktasana practices

The corpus copy lists only an awareness line for each of its 34 Pawanmuktasana practices (17 + 8 + 9): movement, stretch, breath. It names no chakra, mantra or visualisation for them. Its chakra rule and chakra visualisations are in a general chapter, and the children’s edition writes PM1, PM2, PM3. The first edition that the training books mention is not in the corpus.

Where the books differ

  • Prana and Pranayama describes the four-petalled red lotus for mooladhara53; the Practice Guide states that this picture is not the one to use here2.
  • The Pawanmuktasana book ties the squatting asanas of part 3 to the pelvic region and brahma granthi46; the Practice Guide states mooladhara4. The Progressive Yoga Vidya books are the ones that describe this training.

What the corpus does not contain

  • A named mantra for any PM practice. The first edition of Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha, which the Practice Guide mentions28, is not in the corpus.
  • The search covered the English books, magazines and transcripts. Hindi books and magazines were not searched. Pages that are only images, and about 234 books with legacy-font text, cannot be searched as text.
  • 174 passages were collected, and each was checked against the rendered page of the book.
